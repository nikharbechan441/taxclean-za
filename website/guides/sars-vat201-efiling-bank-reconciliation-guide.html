Why Bank Statements Alone Trigger SARS VAT201 Audits

Every two months, registered VAT vendors in South Africa file their VAT201 declaration on SARS eFiling. A common mistake made by small business owners and bookkeepers is pulling bank statement totals into Microsoft Excel and multiplying the total expenditure by 15%. When SARS issues a Request for Relevant Material (Verification Letter), this shortcut leads to massive input tax disallowances, penalties, and interest under the Tax Administration Act.

Under Section 16(2) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991, no input tax deduction may be made unless the vendor possesses a valid full tax invoice (or abridged tax invoice for amounts under R5,000) containing the supplier's 10-digit VAT registration number, physical address, and detailed description of goods or services.

The 4 Statutory Expenditure Buckets on Bank Statements

When reconciling an FNB, Standard Bank, Nedbank, Capitec, or Absa bank statement for VAT201 filing, transactions must be partitioned into four distinct statutory treatments:

Category Statutory Reference Common Examples on Statement VAT201 Treatment
Standard-Rated (15%) Section 7(1)(a) Checkers, Makro, Vodacom, Adobe, Stationery, Subcontractors Claim Gross × 15 / 115 in Field 15 (Input Tax)
Zero-Rated (0%) Section 11(1)(h) Engen, Shell, BP, Sasol, Total (Petrol & Diesel fuel) Include in total expenditure, but claim R 0.00 input VAT
Exempt Supplies Section 12(a) Bank monthly fees, card transaction fees, interest paid, overdraft fees Excluded from VAT claim entirely
Disallowed Supplies Section 17(2) Spur, Mugg & Bean, restaurant meals, staff entertainment, gym fees Input VAT strictly denied by law even if supplier charged 15% VAT

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim VAT back on petrol or diesel bought for company vehicles?
No. Under Section 11(1)(h) of the South African VAT Act, fuel subject to the fuel levy (petrol and diesel) is zero-rated at 0%. No input VAT may be claimed.
What is the formula to calculate 15% VAT from a gross bank statement amount?
To extract VAT from a gross inclusive amount in South Africa, multiply the gross amount by 15 and divide by 115 (Amount * 15 / 115).
Are bank fees subject to VAT in South Africa?
Bank charges, monthly administration fees, and cash handling fees are financial services exempt from VAT under Section 12(a). Do not claim input VAT on bank charges.
