Common Issues When Importing Chase Statements to QuickBooks

JPMorgan Chase is the largest business bank in the United States. While Chase offers direct feeds for some business accounts, feeds frequently disconnect, miss historical transactions over 90 days, or fail for secondary commercial cards. When accountants convert monthly PDF e-statements manually, they hit three persistent issues:

  1. Fragmented Transaction Sections: Chase statements group transactions into distinct sections: Deposits and Additions, ATM & Debit Card Withdrawals, Electronic Withdrawals, and Checks Paid (with separate check number tables). Generic converters scramble the order or drop check numbers completely.
  2. Date Inversion Bugs in Excel: Opening converted CSVs in spreadsheet software often converts US dates (11/05/2026 - November 5) into international format (May 11), creating chaotic reconciliations in QuickBooks.
  3. QuickBooks Desktop .IIF Syntax: QuickBooks Desktop requires strict tab-delimited Intuit Interchange Format (.IIF) headers with specific transaction block markers (!TRNS, !SPL, !ENDTRNS).

QuickBooks Online (QBO) 3-Column CSV Specification

For QuickBooks Online, format your file as a clean 3-column CSV:

Date Description Amount
11/15/2026 STRIPE PAYOUT REF 8901 2450.00
11/16/2026 AMAZON WEB SERVICES -128.50

Frequently Asked Questions

What date format does QuickBooks Online require for US bank imports?
QuickBooks Online in the United States requires MM/DD/YYYY date formatting (e.g. 11/15/2026). Using DD/MM/YYYY will cause dates after the 12th of the month to be rejected.
Can TaxClean convert Chase statements directly to QuickBooks Desktop .IIF?
